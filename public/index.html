FAQs

It's common to think that only people in finance and sales can have a quantifier in their bullets. Although that's not true as every role has something to quantify.

If you think outside of the financial impact of your work, you can find numbers in almost every activity. For example, if you "worked in an international team", you can write "worked in an international team of 50+ people". Using numbers in your resume gives recruiters the ability to grasp the scale you've worked at.

In order to add numbers to your bullets you need to define what exactly can be measured in the activity you've performed and then rewrite the bullet including the metric.

Keep in mind that the quantifier shouldn't always be an amount, it may also be frequency. For example: "Hosted a retrospection meeting every 2 weeks with a team of 10"

    When you're uploading your resume on various platforms like Indeed and LinkedIn, you're likely to meet a file size limit. Ideally, your resume should be less than 2MB in size. Anything larger will most likely not be accepted on the majority of platforms.

    Your file type also plays an important role in ATS screening. PDF files are preferred as the text can be easily read by the applicant tracking system without losing formatting. Avoid DOCX, PNG, or JPG files when a PDF upload is supported.
